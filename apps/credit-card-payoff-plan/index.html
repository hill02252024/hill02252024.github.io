Credit Card Payoff Plan — your debt-free date, and what changes it

Card balances are abstract. A date is not. Add what you owe and this returns the month you finish paying — then lets you push that month closer.

▶ Google Play

Minimum payments hide how long this takes

A credit-card statement tells you the balance, the rate and the minimum payment. What it does not tell you, in any form you can act on, is that paying the minimum on a $6,000 balance at 22% will keep you there for well over a decade. The number that changes behaviour is not the balance — it is the date.

This app exists to produce that date and then make it move. You enter each debt with its balance and rate, choose a strategy, and the countdown at the top of the screen tells you when you are done. Every change you make updates it immediately.

How the debt payoff calculator builds a plan

The whole app is one screen you keep coming back to, plus the pieces that support it.

  • A debt-free date, front and centre. The countdown is the first thing on screen and it is the number the rest of the app serves. It updates the moment you change a balance, add a debt or move the extra-payment slider.
  • Avalanche against snowball, side by side. Avalanche targets the highest interest rate first and costs less; snowball clears the smallest balance first and feels better. A comparison hero shows both outcomes together, with the interest difference, so you can make the trade-off knowingly instead of picking one on faith.
  • An extra-payment slider. Drag it to add $25, $50 or $200 a month and watch the date move. This is the single most persuasive feature in the app — seeing eleven months disappear for the price of a streaming bundle is more motivating than any budgeting advice.
  • A payoff chart and milestones. The balance curve is plotted over time, and milestone badges mark the first $1,000 repaid, then 25%, 50%, 75% and debt free. Clearing an individual card triggers a confetti moment that names the card and the interest you avoided.
  • A monthly reminder. Pick a day of the month and a time and the app will remind you locally to make the payment — scheduled on the device, no notification server involved.

What it deliberately leaves out

There is no account, no bank connection and no offer of a consolidation loan at the end. Debt-payoff calculators are a well-known lead-generation funnel for lenders; this one is not, which is why nothing you enter leaves the phone. Your debts are stored in a local database and the app works with no connection at all.

It also stays deliberately small. It does not try to be a budgeting app or track your spending — it does one calculation, keeps it honest, and makes the result impossible to ignore.

Who it helps

The typical user is carrying two to four balances — a store card, a main credit card, maybe a personal loan — and has never seen them modelled together. Separately each looks manageable; together they have a completion date that is usually further away than expected, and that is the useful shock.

It also helps people who have already decided to attack their debt and want to choose a method. If you have read that avalanche is mathematically superior but suspect snowball will actually keep you going, the comparison screen quantifies what the psychological choice costs you — sometimes it is trivial, sometimes it is hundreds of dollars, and it is worth knowing which.

And it suits anyone about to receive a windfall — a bonus, a tax refund — who wants to see where it does the most good before it disappears.

Privacy and limits

Balances, rates and payments are stored in a database on the phone. There is no sign-up, no sync and no upload. Reminders are scheduled locally.

The projection assumes the rate and payment you enter stay put. Real cards have promotional rates that expire, fees, and interest that compounds daily rather than monthly, so treat the date as a well-informed plan rather than a contract. The app is free and ad-supported, and it does not provide financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between avalanche and snowball? Avalanche pays the highest interest rate first and costs the least overall; snowball clears the smallest balance first so you get a win sooner. The comparison view shows both, including how much the choice costs in interest.

Can I see what paying extra would do? Yes. The extra-payment slider adds a chosen amount per month, and the debt-free countdown updates live as you drag it.

Does it store my balances anywhere online? No. Everything sits in a local database on the phone. There is no account, no sync and nothing uploaded.

Can it remind me to pay? Yes. Pick a day of the month and a time; the reminder is scheduled locally on the device.

How accurate is the debt-free date? It is exact for the inputs you give it. Promotional rates that expire, fees and daily compounding can move the real date, so treat it as a well-informed plan.
