九巴通 長者版 — a Hong Kong bus arrival app in type you can actually read

Built for a specific person: a parent who wants to know how many minutes until the bus, and who cannot read the numbers in most transit apps.

▶ Google Play  App Store

Transit apps are designed for young eyes

The standard Hong Kong bus app packs a dense map, small numerals, a search box and several tabs into a screen designed for someone who can read eight-point type in bright sun. For a large share of the people who most depend on the bus — older residents, several times a day, in weather — that design is a barrier rather than a convenience.

九巴通 長者版 was built from that constraint outwards. The arrival time is the largest thing on the screen. Buttons are big enough to hit with an imprecise tap. And the app can read the arrival time aloud, so it works even when the numbers are still too small.

What the Hong Kong bus arrival app puts on screen

Less on it than a typical transit app, and all of it larger.

  • Arrival times, in large numerals. Live estimates from the government open data feed, presented as a big number with a clear last-updated stamp so you know how fresh it is.
  • Four operators, not just KMB. KMB, Citybus, green minibus and MTR Bus are all covered, because at a real stop in Hong Kong the route you need is often not the one the app's name suggests.
  • Nearby stops, on opening. The app can start at the stops around you rather than at a search box. For a regular trip, that means the answer is on screen before anything is typed.
  • Spoken times. Arrival times can be read aloud. This is the feature that makes the app usable for someone whose eyesight has moved past what any type size can fix.
  • Favourites and route detail. Regular routes are one tap away, and each route opens to its full stop list with the destination shown plainly.
  • Fares and service notices. Fare information for a route, and operator notices surfaced as a banner rather than hidden in a menu.
  • An emergency call button. A dedicated call action, which for the intended audience is a reasonable thing to have on a screen they use daily.
  • QR scanning and boarding. Stop QR codes can be scanned to jump straight to that stop, and a boarding session helps track a journey once you are on.

Accessibility as the brief, not a setting

Many apps offer a large-text option buried in settings that only resizes some of the interface. Here the entire layout is built at that scale — the buttons, the cards, the spacing and the contrast — so nothing breaks when the numbers get big. Custom oversized components are used throughout rather than the default widgets with a font multiplier.

The route and stop database is stored locally, so opening the app and browsing routes does not depend on a connection; only the live arrival estimates need the network.

Who it is for

Older residents who take the bus daily and have been quietly struggling with the mainstream apps. In practice it is often installed by an adult child on a parent's phone, which is why favourites and the nearby-stops-on-open behaviour matter so much: the setup should be done once, by someone else, and then never touched.

It also suits anyone with a visual impairment, anyone using a phone one-handed while carrying shopping, and anyone who simply prefers a bus app that shows one number clearly instead of a map with everything on it.

Data source and disclaimer

Arrival estimates come from Hong Kong's public transport open data, published via data.gov.hk and the operators' own open data endpoints. The app is unofficial and is not affiliated with KMB, Citybus, the MTR Corporation or any minibus operator.

Estimates are predictions from the operators, not guarantees; traffic and cancellations move them. Location is used only to find stops near you. The app is free and ad-supported.

Frequently asked questions

Which bus operators does it cover? KMB, Citybus, green minibus and MTR Bus — all from public open data feeds.

Can it read the arrival time out loud? Yes. Spoken arrival times are built in; that is the point of the elderly edition.

Does it show the stops near me automatically? Yes. It can open on the stops around you rather than a search box, so a regular trip needs no typing at all.

Is it the official KMB app? No. It is unofficial, built on public open data, with no affiliation to KMB, Citybus, the MTR Corporation or any minibus operator.

Does it work without a connection? Route and stop information is stored on the device, so browsing works offline. Live arrival estimates need a connection.
