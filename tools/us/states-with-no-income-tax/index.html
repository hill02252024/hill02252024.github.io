The Nine States With No Income Tax — And What They Charge Instead

Nine states do not tax wage income. That list is stable, short, and widely misread — because "no income tax" describes one line on a paycheck, not a household's total tax burden.

The nine states

These states levy no personal income tax on wages:

StateWhat this means for wages
AlaskaNo state wage income tax and no state sales tax; some boroughs levy local sales tax.
FloridaNo state wage income tax; no municipal wage tax either.
NevadaNo state wage income tax; revenue leans heavily on sales and gaming.
New HampshireNo wage income tax. Its separate tax on interest and dividend income has been repealed. {{NEEDS_VERIFY: effective date and final phase-out year of the New Hampshire interest and dividends tax repeal}}
South DakotaNo state wage income tax.
TennesseeNo wage income tax. Its former Hall tax on investment income has been repealed.
TexasNo state wage income tax; set at constitutional level.
WashingtonNo wage income tax, but a separate tax on certain long-term capital gains above a threshold applies. {{NEEDS_VERIFY: Washington capital gains tax rate, threshold and exemptions}}
WyomingNo state wage income tax.

Two entries on that list carry asterisks that matter. New Hampshire and Tennessee historically taxed investment income rather than wages, and both have retired those taxes — so older articles describing them as partial-income-tax states are out of date. Washington taxes wages not at all but does tax certain capital gains, which makes it the one state on this list where a high earner with equity compensation should not assume a zero state bill.

What "no income tax" does not mean

It does not mean lower total taxes. Every state funds schools, roads, courts and emergency services, and a state that forgoes income tax raises the money through some combination of three other channels.

Sales tax

A consumption tax. It falls harder on people who spend a high share of their income, which in practice means lower earners and large households. A high earner who saves aggressively pays relatively little of it. Alaska is the outlier here — no state sales tax at all, with local option taxes instead.

Property tax

Assessed on the value of real estate rather than on income. This is the biggest structural difference between an income-tax state and a no-income-tax state, and it produces the single most important asymmetry in the whole comparison: a property tax bill does not fall in a year when your income does. An income tax flexes with a bad year, a layoff, or a commission-heavy role having a slow quarter. A property tax bill arrives at the same size regardless.

Industry-specific revenue

Several of these states have a revenue source that ordinary states do not. Alaska has resource royalties, Nevada has gaming, Florida has tourism. This is why the list is stable: these states are not making a temporary political choice, they have a structural alternative. It also means the model is not portable — a state without that alternative cannot simply copy it.

Who actually comes out ahead

The swap from income tax to property and sales tax has predictable winners and losers.

  • Clear win: high earner who rents. You avoid the income tax entirely and dodge most of the property tax. The saving is close to a pure gain.
  • Clear win: someone with volatile income. Commission, contract or equity-heavy earners benefit from having their largest tax not indexed to a good year.
  • Roughly neutral: median earner who buys a house. The property tax bill replaces much of what the income tax would have been, and the sales tax picks up the rest.
  • Possible loss: modest earner with a large family and a house. Consumption is high relative to income, the property tax applies in full, and the income tax that was avoided would have been small anyway because of graduated brackets and the standard deduction.

That last case is the one the "move to a no-tax state" advice consistently gets wrong. Progressive income tax systems already tax modest incomes lightly. Trading a small income tax bill for a full property tax bill is not obviously a good trade.

Retirees are a separate case

For someone drawing on retirement income rather than wages, the calculation changes. A state with an income tax may exempt Social Security benefits, pension income, or retirement account distributions in whole or in part — which can leave a retiree paying little state income tax even in a state that has one. Meanwhile the property tax in a no-income-tax state applies at full strength to a paid-off house. {{NEEDS_VERIFY: which income-tax states fully exempt Social Security benefits and retirement distributions, and any age-based property tax relief in the nine no-income-tax states}}

How to check your own case

  1. Compute federal income tax and FICA with the take-home salary calculator. This is identical in all fifty states and is usually the largest deduction — knowing it first stops you overestimating what the state layer is worth.
  2. Look up the state income tax you would actually pay in your current state, at your income and filing status. Use the effective rate, not the top marginal rate.
  3. Get a real property tax estimate for a specific property in the destination — not a state average. Assessment practice varies enormously by county.
  4. Add a realistic annual sales tax estimate based on your actual spending, not on income.
  5. For Florida specifically, get a homeowners insurance quote for the county. It is not a tax, but it behaves like one in the budget.

Frequently asked questions

Which states have no income tax?

Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming levy no personal income tax on wages. New Hampshire and Tennessee previously taxed investment income and have repealed those taxes; Washington taxes certain long-term capital gains above a threshold but does not tax wages.

Do I pay less total tax in a state with no income tax?

Not necessarily. Those states raise revenue through sales tax, property tax and industry-specific sources instead. A high earner who rents usually comes out ahead; a median earner who buys a house often comes out roughly even, and can come out behind once property tax and sales tax are counted.

Is New Hampshire still taxing interest and dividends?

No. New Hampshire's tax on interest and dividend income has been repealed, which is a change from how the state was described for many years. Articles that still list New Hampshire as a partial income tax state are out of date.

Does Washington really have no income tax?

Washington does not tax wage income. It does levy a tax on certain long-term capital gains above a threshold, so someone whose compensation is heavily equity-based should not assume a zero state bill there.

Why is Alaska different from the other eight?

Alaska has no state sales tax either, which makes it the only state on the list without both. Local boroughs may levy their own sales tax. Its revenue base rests substantially on resource royalties rather than on taxing residents.
